01

Bestfrend

One of one. Handmade. Through thick and thin.
Own brand · 2018 to present
Brand · Product · E-commerce

The apparel and art brand I started in 2018 and have run every part of since. It is where I learned everything it takes to start and run a clothing brand, by doing all of it.

Role: founder. Brand, design, production, photography, store, sales and fulfillment. bestfrend.xyz
Hand-painted suit, shot in the studio
Where it started

Bend, 2018. No team and no budget, a sewing machine, a screen and a point of view. Bestfrend began as the clothes I wanted to wear and could not buy, and a name that says what the brand is for: there is no I in frend.

How it is made

Every piece is one of one. Garments and fabric are sourced, cut, sewn, printed, painted and patched by hand, then shot in-house, listed, packed and shipped by me. Print-on-demand came in later for secondary products, which is where my POD and Shopify fluency started.

What it became

My primary income from 2019 to 2022 across Bend, Fort Lauderdale and San Diego: drops, pop-ups, a store window, and a site where nothing is available anywhere else. The brand taught me product, production, pricing, e-commerce, content, fulfillment and community, all at once.

Bear Prints becomes BRANDLAB

A Central Oregon print shop operating since 1978, rebuilt from the name up.
Rebrand · 2022 to 2026
Rebrand · Retail · Leadership

I set the vision, created the name, logo, slogan and business model, and carried the identity through to a new retail location that opened February 2, 2026. I run it now as general manager.

Role: general manager and creative lead. Name, logo, slogan, identity system, environment, web, collateral, launch, and the team of seven that runs it.
BRANDLAB, Redmond, the night before opening
What they had

Bear Prints had printed shirts for Central Oregon since 1978 and had worn three different logos doing it. People knew the name. The name did not say what the shop had become: apparel, print, embroidery, promo, and online company stores for local businesses.

The idea

Stop selling printing and sell the brand. A new name, BRANDLAB, a monogram that works as a hat patch and a storefront, and a slogan that says the whole offer in three words: Design. Create. Wear. The business model followed the name: a retail-facing shop that builds brands and the merch that carries them.

What shipped

The mark, the identity system, the window and counter wrap, the hiring flyer, business cards and in-store print, the online store, and a built-out retail floor that opened on February 2, 2026. I managed the launch and lead the team of seven that runs it.

Buckstop

A truck outfitter that had worn the same oval badge for two decades.
Rebrand · Buckstop Truckware, est. 2001
Rebrand · Merch program

The new mark finally looks like the trucks they build: BUCKSTOP arched over a lifted Super Duty wearing their front bumper, mountains behind it.

Role: creative lead and account lead. Logo, color, apparel graphics, online store, merch program, quoting and production.
Printed tee, truck back
What they had

Buckstop has built bumpers and outfitted trucks in Oregon since 2001. For most of that time the brand was a red-and-grey oval, with TrailReady sub-brands for wheels and bumpers alongside it. Solid, forgettable, and nothing you would wear.

The idea

Put the name where the truck already is. One version in red and green for the drop; one in a single color for everything that gets printed, embroidered or wrapped after.

What shipped

The drop, the online store, printed tees, and the truck-back graphic: suspension, bumpers, wheels. A mark their customers ask for on a shirt, which is the whole point.

Otto’s Landing

A bar mark built for a leather patch first.
Identity and merch · Redmond
Identity · Merch

The owner wanted something people would wear home. So the mark was designed for a two-inch leather patch before anything else: a wood slice with the OTO monogram cut into it. Hats, tees, stickers and vinyl followed from that.

Role: identity, patch, apparel graphics, merch program, quoting and production.
The patch on the hat
The brief

A neighborhood bar in Redmond with regulars, a river name and no identity. The ask was simple: make something people will actually want on a hat.

The idea

Design for the hardest surface first. A leather patch is small, one color and debossed, so the mark had to be a shape, not a drawing: a wood slice with the name cut straight across the rings, and an OTO monogram for the places even that will not fit. If it works on the patch it works everywhere.

What shipped

The badge and the OTO monogram, leather patch hats, tees in four colorways, cut vinyl for the windows, sticker runs, and the proofs that keep the reorders consistent.

Triple D

Concept to press for a landscape crew.
Identity and merch · Landscape maintenance
Identity · Merch

Triple D Landscape Maintenance needed a mark that would survive a jobsite: hats, hoodies, jackets, truck decals and the stickers on the cutter. The logo, the proofs, the hats and jackets, the printed hoodie coming off the press.

Role: identity, apparel graphics, decals, merch program, production.
Printed hoodie, off the press
The brief

A growing crew in trucks all day. The mark had to read on a hat from across a yard and still embroider clean at two inches.

The idea

Three Ds locked inside a circle at the head of a rake, bold enough for a truck door and simple enough for a one-color print. Built as a system: the mark alone, the mark with the name, and a stacked version for the back of a hoodie.

What shipped

Hats and jackets, printed hoodies, stickers and decals, and the proof set that every reorder runs from. From the first sketch to the first pull on press.

Spirit of Our Native Language

Drawn by hand, with elders, during the event.
Nonprofit · Warm Springs
Nonprofit · Illustration

An event and nonprofit for Native language revitalization. I sat with elders from the reservation and drew the mark there: a face speaking, the language inside it.

Role: illustrator and creative lead. Hand-drawn mark, annual identity, apparel, patches.
Printed tees and patches, stacked for the gathering
What they had

A gathering on the Warm Springs reservation for people keeping their languages alive, and a need for an identity that came from the community rather than being handed to it.

The idea

Draw it there. The badge comes back every year with a new treatment, so it stays recognizable while each year is its own.

What shipped

The 2022 Spirit of Our Native Language badge, the 2023 Healing Through Our Native Languages version, the Eagles Ichishkiin tee for Warm Springs, and printed tees and patches for every attendee, every year.
